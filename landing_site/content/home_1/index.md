<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>The Australian Acoustic Observatory (A2O) is a continental-scale acoustic sensor network, recording for a five-year period across multiple Australian ecosystems.</strong> </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A2O will have approximately 360 continuously operating acoustic sensors collecting  2PB of sound data over the duration of the project. The  project is funded by an <a rel="noreferrer noopener" href="https://www.arc.gov.au/" target="_blank"><strong>Australian Research Council</strong></a> (ARC) Linkage Infrastructure, Equipment and Facilities (LIEF) grant of $1.8 million.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>More information about the A2O can be found in a paper published in <a href="/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/A2O_Methods-in-Ecology-and-Evolution_2021.pdf" data-type="URL" data-id="/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/A2O_Methods-in-Ecology-and-Evolution_2021.pdf"><strong>Methods in Ecology and Evolution (Roe at al., 2021</strong>)</a>. Please cite this paper if using A2O data for research or other purposes.</p>

<h2><strong>Why have an observatory?</strong></h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:block {"ref":2059} /-->

<!-- wp:spacer {"height":30} -->
<div style="height:30px" aria-hidden="true" class="wp-block-spacer"></div>
<!-- /wp:spacer -->

<!-- wp:media-text {"mediaPosition":"right","mediaId":1742,"mediaType":"image","mediaWidth":52,"backgroundColor":"very-light-gray"} -->
<div class="wp-block-media-text alignwide has-media-on-the-right is-stacked-on-mobile has-very-light-gray-background-color has-background" style="grid-template-columns:auto 52%"><figure class="wp-block-media-text__media"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/cunnamulla-arid-image.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-1742 size-full"/></figure><div class="wp-block-media-text__content"><!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size"><strong>A2O is leading collaborative research</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The A2O is led by Professor Paul Roe from the Queensland University of Technology (QUT) Ecoacoustics Research Group, in collaboration with Professor David Watson from Charles Sturt University (CSU), Professor Lin Schwarzkopf from James Cook University (JCU), Associate Professor Paul McDonald from the University of New England (UNE), and Professor Richard Fuller from the University of Queensland (UQ).</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The A2O <a href="http://acousticobservatory.org/affiliated-researchers/"><strong>Chief Investigators</strong></a><strong> </strong> have a deep understanding of Australia’s fragile and mega-diverse  environment, and the collection and interpretation of acoustic data,  providing leading research expertise in this emerging field.</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph {"backgroundColor":"very-light-gray"} -->
<p class="has-very-light-gray-background-color has-background">Not unlike astronomical observatories, the A2O collects large volumes of high-resolution data over time using a distributed sensor network.</p>

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2><strong>A2O design</strong></h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:media-text {"mediaPosition":"right","mediaId":187,"mediaType":"image","mediaWidth":53} -->
<div class="wp-block-media-text alignwide has-media-on-the-right is-stacked-on-mobile" style="grid-template-columns:auto 53%"><figure class="wp-block-media-text__media"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/map_v2.png" alt="" class="wp-image-187 size-full"/></figure><div class="wp-block-media-text__content"><!-- wp:paragraph {"backgroundColor":"very-light-gray","fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-very-light-gray-background-color has-background has-medium-font-size"><strong>Site locations</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"backgroundColor":"very-light-gray"} -->
<p class="has-very-light-gray-background-color has-background">The A2O incorporates 90 sites across seven major ecoregions. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></div></div>
<!-- /wp:media-text -->

<!-- wp:media-text {"mediaPosition":"right","mediaId":1589,"mediaType":"image","mediaWidth":52} -->
<div class="wp-block-media-text alignwide has-media-on-the-right is-stacked-on-mobile" style="grid-template-columns:auto 52%"><figure class="wp-block-media-text__media"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/a20-design-summary_032019_v3.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-1589 size-full"/></figure><div class="wp-block-media-text__content"><!-- wp:paragraph {"placeholder":"Content…","backgroundColor":"very-light-gray","fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-very-light-gray-background-color has-background has-medium-font-size"><strong>Site-level design</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"backgroundColor":"very-light-gray"} -->
<p class="has-very-light-gray-background-color has-background">Each <strong><a href="/sites/">site</a> </strong>is  linked to four acoustic sensors, with two acoustic sensors  established in relatively wet habitat (wetland, river, creek, drainage  line, spring, depression etc.) and two in relatively dry habitat. The  range of wet and dry locations varies from site to site based on local  conditions. This arrangement provides a total of 360&nbsp;acoustic sensors  across the network.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></div></div>
<!-- /wp:media-text -->

<!-- wp:spacer {"height":20} -->
<div style="height:20px" aria-hidden="true" class="wp-block-spacer"></div>

<p class="has-very-light-gray-background-color has-background has-medium-font-size"><strong>Hardware</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"backgroundColor":"very-light-gray"} -->
<p class="has-very-light-gray-background-color has-background">Acoustic sensors are built to specification by <a rel="noreferrer noopener" aria-label="Frontier Labs (opens in a new tab)" href="https://frontierlabs.com.au/" target="_blank"><strong>Frontier Labs</strong></a> in Brisbane, Australia. Each acoustic sensor stores data on high-capacity SD cards, which are manually collected and replaced at least once a year.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"backgroundColor":"very-light-gray"} -->
<p class="has-very-light-gray-background-color has-background">Acoustic sensors are powered by solar panels, with all equipment easily mounted on a standard 1.8 m star picket. This design ensures each acoustic sensor and additional hardware is simple to <a href="/deployment-information/"><strong>install </strong></a>and has minimal space requirements. </p>
