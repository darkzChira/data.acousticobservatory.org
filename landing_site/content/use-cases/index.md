<!-- wp:heading {"level":4,"style":{"elements":{"link":{"color":{"text":"var:preset|color|black"}}}},"textColor":"black"} -->

<!-- /wp:heading --></div>
<!-- /wp:column -->

<!-- wp:column -->
<div class="wp-block-column"><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>To determine the ability of the A2O to be useful for monitoring threatened species, we are currently searching all sites for the presence of vocal vertebrate species listed on the EPBC Act List of Threatened Fauna. This work is a follow-up to our recently published research note in Austral Ecology (<em>Can an acoustic observatory contribute to the conservation of threatened species?</em> See:&nbsp;<a href="https://urldefense.com/v3/__https://doi.org/10.1111/aec.13398__;!!NVzLfOphnbDXSw!C4oZew8u1cN8jkDH0JpUT3x5lQxzZosKL2YxpG3V9afb-5BVSMEYWeeCRmyYK-0q1utKU-uA6wwyKdSDGy4W-_YAL-3hLSUDZRon$" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">https://doi.org/10.1111/aec.13398</a>).</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We have also developed call recognisers for the Red fox and the Dingo and are investigating their distribution across A2O sites as well as potential interactions between the two species and environmental drivers of their occurrence.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
